I have been on a years-long quest to unplug myself from the internet (but, keeping everything that's useful about the internet).

I started with the Light Phone II which is on its own a very interesting device (I might write another article on it someday). The LPII is great for everyday phone tasks, and I even replaced my phone entirely with it, but this hasn't stopped me from spending hours of screen time each day. It certainly reduced my screen time, but it mostly moved my problems to my desktop.

I've been thinking about this a lot lately, inspired by the recent _On The Media_ episode interviewing Cory Doctorow, the journalist who coined the term "enshittification." What I began to realize is that no matter what I do, I will at some point need to access a desktop-class browser and social media apps. As much as I would like to quit social media entirely, it is so intertwined with the ways in which I communicate with friends and family. This is completely on purpose and is in fact part of the whole reason that, for example, Facebook bought Instagram: They saw users migrating to Instagram because people liked using it and their friends were using it, and they wanted to make sure that where people went to connect with their friends, Facebook's tracking and advertising business could follow.

It wasn't until I was reading before bed (_How to Love Better_ by Yung Pueblo, if you are curious) that I realized I held in my hands the perfect device to achieve this goal: The Barnes & Noble Nook GlowLight 4. I thought about the Boox Palma, the Android phone from E-reader company Boox, which I decided against when I got my LPII due to it's lack of cellular data i.e. texts and calls.
