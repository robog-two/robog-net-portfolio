# VOICE.md — How Sam's sentences should sound

A sentence-level vocal score for the robog.net blog, calibrated from published posts
(`src/blog/`). This is about sound, not substance — what the article must *do* lives in
[STYLE.md](./STYLE.md).

Sam uses any/all pronouns.

## The overall sound

Talk to the reader like a smart friend who's genuinely excited about the thing. Warm,
direct, and a little playful — willing to be earnest, willing to be blunt. The prose
should feel spoken aloud, not composed. When in doubt, read it out loud; if it doesn't
sound like a person talking, cut it.

## Cadence and sentence length

Vary hard. Let explanatory sentences run long and accumulate — especially when walking
through how something works — then snap to a very short declarative or a fragment for
impact.

- Long, gathering: *"So even though we developed this complex system for recommending
  artists... the problem was we just didn't have enough local artists to recommend you.
  We needed more."*
- Then the snap: *"We needed more."* / *"It's just a large language model."* /
  *"Posted. Boom."*

One-line paragraphs are a signature move. Use them to land a point, pivot, or reveal:
*"I know this because I learned how to draw."*

## Syntax and recurring turns

- **Rhetorical questions as pivots.** Ask the reader the question, then answer it. Often
  starts a new beat: *"So, is this evil?"* / *"So what's inside our magic black box?"* /
  *"Right?"* Good for section openings and for turning a corner in an argument.
- **Anaphora for rhythm and stakes.** Repeat an opening clause to build:
  *"We haven't seen AI cure cancer. We haven't seen AI music that is incredibly
  interesting... We haven't seen AI art..."* and *"It's about combining... It's about
  expanding..."* Use sparingly, at emotional peaks.
- **Direct address.** Speak to "you," and narrate your own move when it helps: *"The
  reason I'm telling you this is because..."*
- **Em-dashes and parentheticals for asides** — drop in the aside in the writer's own
  voice, then keep going. *"(no promises >:])"*, *"—they don't make music full-time."*

## Diction and register

- Casual and contracted throughout: *kind of*, *gonna*, *stuff*, *a lot of*, *really*,
  *honestly*. Don't formalize these away.
- Plain verbs and concrete nouns over abstraction. Sam explains a fan controller, a
  data pipeline, or a drawing class in the same grounded, physical language.
- **Emotional honesty, and swear when it's earned.** *"that is complete and utter
  bullshit"*, *"It's honestly embarrassing."* Strong language is a deliberate tool for
  the from-the-heart posts, not a tic — see the footnote in "Learning to Draw" that
  flags it on purpose.
- Enthusiasm is allowed to show: exclamation points where the excitement is real
  (*"It's fun!"*), and the occasional emoji as a sign-off flourish (*"— Sam 💙"*).

## Punctuation and texture

- Italics do two jobs: emphasis (*"*And that's a problem.*"*) and a wry stage-direction
  aside (*"*This blog post is adapted from a keynote.*"*, the *artificial intelephant*).
- Self-aware meta-commentary is welcome, including in HTML comments and footnotes:
  *"Yes there is HTML in my markdown. Yes it is gross. I needed a diagram, though!"*
- Sign off with a flourish when the piece wants one: *"Thank you."* / *"Posted. Boom."*
  / *"— Sam 💙"*. Don't force it on every post.

## Humor and self-awareness

Self-deprecating, never self-important. Sam pokes at their own perfectionism and their
own process rather than at the reader. Jokes land through specificity and understatement,
not through being told they're jokes.

## Revision checklist (preserve the sound without copying phrases)

- [ ] Does it read like Sam talking out loud? Kill anything that sounds like a press
      release or an essay-for-a-grade.
- [ ] Is the length varied — long explanatory runs broken by short punches? Add a
      one-line paragraph where a point deserves to land.
- [ ] Are there stiff academic connectors (*furthermore*, *moreover*, *in conclusion*)?
      Replace with a direct turn, a question, or nothing.
- [ ] Are contractions and casual diction intact? Don't let a polish pass formalize the
      voice out.
- [ ] Where the post is personal, is the honesty real — including a willingness to be
      blunt or to swear when it means it? Don't sand down the edges.
- [ ] Is enthusiasm present where it's genuine, and absent where it'd be fake?
- [ ] Do NOT copy the excerpts above verbatim. They're directional — they show the move,
      not phrases to reuse.
