## Corrections to draft-part1.md

- **"Indeed buying Glassdoor": needs correction.** Recruit Holdings (Indeed's parent)
  bought Glassdoor for $1.2B in 2018, and Glassdoor ran as a sister company. The
  integration came later:
  - July 2025: about 1,300 layoffs across Indeed and Glassdoor.
  - Nov 2025: new users sign in with an Indeed account.
  - 1 July 2026: Glassdoor LLC legally merged into Indeed, Inc.
  - The effect on review and salary independence is opinion. No evidence was found
    either way.
  - Sources: https://recruit-holdings.com/en/newsroom/20180509_8170/ ·
    https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/11/indeed-glassdoor-to-lay-off-1300-staff/ ·
    https://www.reworked.co/employee-experience/glassdoor-folds-into-indeed/
- **"Record high churn": inaccurate now.** JOLTS Aug 2026 (released 2026-09-29): quits
  1.9%, hires 3.3%, separations 3.2%.
  - The record was the 2021 Great Resignation: quits 3.0%.
  - Today is described as "low hire, low fire."
  - Source: https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/jolts_09292026.htm
- **"They find out they hate their jobs": unsupported as a general claim.** Closest data:
  - Gallup H1 2026: 31% of US workers engaged, 18% actively disengaged, down from 36%
    engaged in 2020. https://www.gallup.com/workplace/712433/employee-engagement-remains-flat-adoption-accelerates.aspx
  - Conference Board 2025: overall job satisfaction is at a record high, but only 57.4%
    of workers under 25 are satisfied (vs 72.4% of those 55+), and under-25 was the only
    group that declined. https://www.conference-board.org/topics/job-satisfaction/press/job-satisfaction-2025
- **Handshake stale "fresh new jobs" and LinkedIn Premium pop-ups:** personal anecdote,
  so keep them framed as your experience. Ghost-job figures are all secondary:
  - Greenhouse: 18–22% of postings are ghost jobs.
  - LiveCareer: 93% of HR professionals say their employer posts them.
- **ATS keyword bots:** Sam's skepticism is directionally supported, but the sources are
  weak (resume-builder blogs). The real auto-filter is knockout questions.
- **AI mass-applying:** supported.
  - Greenhouse 2025: 74% of job seekers use AI tools, and 49% sent more applications.
    The CEO calls it an "AI doom loop." https://fortune.com/2026/07/27/greenhouse-ceo-daniel-chait-ai-doom-loop-job-seekers-spam-interview-applications-unemployment/
  - LinkedIn: about 11,000 applications per minute (secondary).
    https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/29/recruiters-are-drinking-through-a-fire-hose-of-job-applications-experts-say.html

- Handshake's own Class of 2026 outlook: postings down more than 16% year over year,
  applications per job up 26% (confirm in the PDF).
  https://joinhandshake.com/themes/handshake/dist/assets/downloads/network-trends/class-of-2026-outlook.pdf

## Weak ties
- Granovetter, "The Strength of Weak Ties," *AJS* 78(6), May 1973, pp. 1360–1380.
  https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/225469
  - Of those who found jobs through contacts (N=54), 16.7% saw the contact often, 55.6%
    occasionally, 27.8% rarely (secondary).
- *Getting a Job* (1974): most professional and technical job changers heard about their
  job through personal contacts, often acquaintances.
- Rajkumar et al., *Science* 377(6612), Sept 2022: randomized LinkedIn experiments on
  20M+ people.
  - Causal support for weak ties, but the effect is an inverted U: moderately weak ties
    help most, and the very weakest help less. The effect is stronger in digital
    industries.
  - https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abl4476
- **Don't cite "70–85% of jobs come from networking."** It traces to an informal 2016
  LinkedIn poll and a 1966 study.

## Hire-source data (old, and it cuts both ways)
- Lever, 2015–16, 4M+ candidates (via SHRM):
  - Direct applicants were 50% of hires and referrals 16%.
  - Referrals converted 1 in 16, direct applicants 1 in 152.
  - So most hires still come from applications, but referrals are about 10x more
    efficient per attempt.
  - https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/talent-acquisition/lever-study-shows-1-100-candidates-hired
- SilkRoad 2016: referrals were over 30% of hires.

  as a comment on incentives (employers and schools pay, and it has pivoted into AI data

## Networking and privilege (added for the close)
Found by Claude with web search, via summaries. Read the primaries before citing.

- **First-gen students:** they have fewer professional connections and a harder time
  getting degree-level jobs. They're more likely to work or commute, which leaves less
  time for internships and clubs. Having senior professionals in your network is reported
  to matter more than GPA, major, or prior internships.
  https://feed.georgetown.edu/access-affordability/first-gen-grads-getting-less-out-of-their-job-search-research-says/
  · https://www.the74million.org/article/who-you-know-social-capital-is-key-for-first-gen-students-career-success/
- **Chetty et al., *Nature* 2022 (21 billion Facebook friendships):** "economic
  connectedness" (friendships across class lines) is the strongest predictor of upward
  mobility. About half of the gap comes from "friending bias" (people befriend others like
  themselves even when exposed to a mix of people), not just exposure.
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-04997-3
  · https://opportunityinsights.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/socialcapital_nontech.pdf
- **Referrals and homophily:** referrals tend to reproduce the referrer's demographics,
  which can widen employment gaps between groups. The evidence is mixed on whether cutting
  referrals helps. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378873317303088
