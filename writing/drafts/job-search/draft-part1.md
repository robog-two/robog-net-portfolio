I was talking to a colleague today when I heard the career center at my college 
recommends applying to **30-50 jobs per day** as a senior. To me, this was a really 
shocking number. I probably could not apply to 30 jobs in a day even if I had the whole 
day to myself, locked in a room with no responsibilities.

I think a lot of career advice I hear these days feels just like this. "No one reads 
anything," "just submit a bunch of applications with AI," "tailor your resume with 
keywords so the machine picks it up." In this era of automation and economic upheaval, 
people are trying desperately to figure out how to get a job in the old system when, in 
fact, the old system is in the process of falling apart.

Then there's the grifters: Handshake in my emails sending me "fresh new jobs" that are 
no longer accepting responses with a posting that is woefully out of date, Indeed buying 
Glassdoor and ruining any chance they have of independently and honestly discussing 
salary/working conditions, and finally the endless LinkedIn premium pop-ups. Maybe the 
best way to make money is to not get a job, but to tell people that you have jobs for 
them... If only they buy our new subscription offering!

I don't want to say that all of this advice, or all of these tools are completely 
useless. I certainly tailor my resume based on the application, but I don't do it to 
fight keyword bots. I think what this kind of career advice misses is that one of the 
most important things as a young person is finding a job you actually want to work. 
You need to have a clear destination, and you need to take steps towards that dream 
career.

I want to provide a version of career advice that has been most effective in my 
anecdotal experience for finding a job that is specific and related to what you are 
looking for.  In my experience as a college student navigating the job market, the best 
method for finding work you love is about setting yourself up to find people, giving 
them an opportunity to reach out to you, and having a place to give prospective 
recruiters a way to understand who you are and what you stand for.
