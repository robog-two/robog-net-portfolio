**Form:** Numbered claims, modeled on `src/docs/generative-ai-usage.md`. Bold one-line
summary, short intro, numbered sections whose headings are full claims with an italic
key word, a bold final note, a warm sign-off, and a note on how it was written.

## Intro: taking apart the 30–50 a day advice
**Main point:** This advice is insane, and here's why.
**Hook:** The career center's 30–50 applications a day.
**Beats:**
- What you heard, when, and from whom.
- The math: one to two per hour, and where that time goes.
- What mass-applying produces: slop jobs, email blasting, "I guess this person looks
  fine."
- Who this is for: students hearing this and feeling like they're always getting
  rejected.
- "Let's take this apart." Set up the numbered sections.
- (Optional, mirrors the AI policy's caveat) Applications and resumes are "good things."
  This post isn't about throwing them out.
**Promise:** A different way to find work, from your own experience.

## 1. The best jobs you *don't apply to* (Sam)
**Main point:** The best jobs come from someone who's running the thing finding out
about you.
**Beats:**
- Story 1: Doug Turnbull and the music research internship.
- Story 2: the chapel, the business card, and the website visit.
- What they share: neither was someone you knew well. Weak ties (Granovetter).
- Leverage: your site shows how you see yourself, not the "information box."
**Bottom line:** Having people come to you is the "true power."

## 2. Networking is just *talking to people* (Sam)
**Main point:** Networking isn't suits and corporate events.
**Beats:**
- The myth: the fancied-up business word.
- What it is: telling people in your life what you're looking for and asking if they
  know anything.
- Who: a professor, a friend of a friend, your uncle who goes to conferences.
- Being involved in things (clubs, showing up) puts you in the room.
**Bottom line:** You already have the people.

## 3. Give them a way to *come back to you* (Sam)
**Main point:** This is the difference between networking and just making friends.
**Beats:**
- The lead: business card, website page, blog post, portfolio, GitHub.
- Most people already have these things. Put them in "a cohesive space."
- Your resume is "just another link on my website."
- For the coffee-shop student: "here's six links you can look at."
**Bottom line:** Make yourself noticeable, and make yourself findable.

## 4. Start small and let it *compound* [model label, "compounds on itself" is Sam's]
**Main point:** It didn't happen overnight, and it doesn't have to.
**Beats:**
- Don't build a giant hub. Start with an about page and a few links.
- Add a blog when you want to share ideas. Add a portfolio when you want to share
  drawings. Print the portfolio when a job asks.
- Be opportunistic: you've done a thing, now share it.
- Tools don't matter: 11ty templates or whatever. Even LinkedIn or Instagram with an
  email and a portfolio counts.
- Honesty beat: people say your site is nice, but you think it needs so much work.
- The ladder: links lead to internships, which lead to polished resume items.
**Bottom line:** A little at a time adds up.
