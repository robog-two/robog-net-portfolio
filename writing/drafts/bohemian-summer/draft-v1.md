Here is what I did with a good chunk of my summer: I sat by the pool, in the sun, reading David Sedaris, eating pretzels and hummus.

That's it. That's the achievement.

The hummus was the good kind, the kind you are not supposed to buy because you can "just make it yourself," which is true and also not going to happen. The pretzels were the little rods, for structural reasons — a rod is a delivery system, a chip is a gamble. I read until the pages got warm. I got up when I felt like it. I am a vegetarian, so nothing on this blanket had ever caused anyone any trouble, and I would like that noted for the record.

Somebody smarter than me has surely run a study on whether this is a productive use of a young person's summer. I did not look it up. You can follow every study in the world and still end up the sort of person who only goes outside in a sun hat — informed, protected, and having no fun at all. It's not very healthy, having fun. But it is, I've decided, extremely important to living a rich and full life.

So I set out to have a rich and full life. Here is roughly how it went.

## The board

At some point every serious hedonist has to build a charcuterie board, which is a fancy word for "dinner that is also a craft project." Mine, for the record:

- A soft cheese and a sharp one, because a board needs an argument
- Some kind of fig or apricot jam, to referee that argument
- Something briny — olives, cornichons, whatever was making eyes at me
- Good bread, torn, never sliced
- Grapes, which I do not need and will not apologize for
- One fancy thing bought purely so I could say "oh, you have to try this"

I could tell you a board is about balance and texture and blah blah blah. What it is actually about is this: a rich life is mostly a matter of what you're willing to put on the board. Nobody is stopping you. The grapes are three dollars.

## Going alone

The other thing I got very good at this summer was leaving.

"Sorry, I can't right now — I have to go run off into the woods." I said versions of this out loud, to real people, and I meant it. I got nomadic. Independent to the point of being slightly feral. I went to the gallery by myself. I went to shows by myself. I went to Barnes & Noble and read and studied like it was 2009 and I was avoiding someone.

This is the part where I'm supposed to worry that I'm retreating into myself. That going alone is just loneliness with better branding. And sure — some Mondays it's too hot and you go to Dollar Tree and you buy things you don't need and you end up talking about nail polish with a woman named Nicole who works there, and on the drive home a little voice says *this is the life of a person who is failing.*

But then you consider that you just had a genuinely lovely conversation about nail polish with Nicole, who is delightful, and the voice doesn't really have a rebuttal.

You can be your own plus-one. It is much better than it sounds.

## Making things by hand

I got really into drawing.

Not for anything. That's the important part. Not to post, not to sell, not to prove the thing I keep trying to prove to people who once told me I didn't have the spark. Just charcoal, and paper, and the specific animal quiet of trying to get a shadow right.

Turns out the point of a craft can just be the doing of it. You are allowed to make something that goes nowhere. I did, all summer, and I'd recommend it.

## Reading the planets

I also got deeply, unrepentantly into astrology.

I know. I *know.* But hear me out, because I've thought about this more than is strictly reasonable, which is sort of the whole point.

I am obsessed with learning, and I have a Virgo moon. I am obsessed with marriage — the idea of it, the enormity of it — and I have Venus in the seventh house. Do these facts cause each other? Absolutely not, and I don't care. What astrology gives me isn't a diagnosis; a diagnosis files you under a label and hands you a pamphlet. This is warmer than that. It's a language of symbols for things that are true about me but too big and too mine to fit in the clinical boxes. It's helped me understand my relationships, my parents, even how to feel about the news without drowning in it.

Evidence, I've come to believe, is not that important for living a good life. A good symbol holds a truth better than a study ever could, because the study is about people in general and the symbol is about *you.* You couldn't test any of this in a lab. That was never the assignment.

## Only from the inside

Here's the thing I noticed, near the end, once the tan had set in.

The pool, the board, the solo shows, the drawing, the birth chart — every good thing I did this summer had the same shape. Each one was completely logical from where I was sitting and completely baffling from anywhere else. Internally coherent, externally ridiculous. There's a word for that, if you want one: esoteric. A little world with its own rules, legible only to the person living in it.

I don't think it needs to make sense to you. I think that was always the point.

It wasn't healthy. It was rich and full, which is a different thing, and a better one.

Now if you'll excuse me — the hummus isn't going to eat itself.
