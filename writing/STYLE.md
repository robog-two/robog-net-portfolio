# STYLE.md — What a robog.net blog post must do

What a post has to accomplish, contain, and prove. How the sentences *sound* lives in
[VOICE.md](./VOICE.md).

## Audience and promise

The blog is Sam's personal space, explicitly *"from the heart, not for the résumé."*
Readers are a mix: curious general readers, technical peers, and people who wandered over
from LinkedIn or GitHub. Write for the smart general reader first; peers can keep up.

The standing promise, set in the first-ever post: posts here will **not** be exhaustively
researched, heavily cited, and agonized over. They will be **interesting and new** — and,
above all, they will *exist*. Shipping an interesting, useful, honest post beats
polishing a perfect one into oblivion.

## The core standard: publish over perfect

Sam's default failure mode is perfectionism — holding work back because it doesn't meet
"unreasonably strict standards." The blog exists to fight that. So:

- **Done and posted beats ideal and unpublished.** If it's interesting and honest, it's
  ready enough.
- Don't gate a post on more research, more citations, or more polish than the idea needs.
- A post can be short. "Learning to Draw" and "Digital Minimalism" earn their length by
  saying one true thing well.

## What every post should have

- **A hook that starts in the concrete** — a personal story, a direct question, or a
  vivid claim. Not a throat-clearing intro. (See the confession that opens "Learning to
  Draw," or *"Let's address the artificial intelephant in the room."*)
- **A clear takeaway.** The reader should finish knowing the one thing you wanted them to
  get. State it plainly when it helps — an explicit *"Moral of the story:"* is on-brand.
- **A reason it's useful or interesting to someone else.** Sam's framing: *"Since I had to
  struggle through a problem, that means someone else won't need to."* Personal essays
  clear a different bar — they need to be *true*, not useful.

## Two post shapes

**Personal / reflective essay** (Learning to Draw, First Ever Blog Post):
- Open with a specific moment or admission.
- Move through the thinking honestly, including the uncomfortable parts.
- Land on a takeaway that's earned, not tacked on.

**Technical / project write-up** (fan controller, Podman, human-app keynote):
- State plainly what the thing should do, or the question being answered.
- Explain why existing options fall short (the *why build this* beat).
- Walk through how it works — use numbered steps, bullets, and diagrams freely; clarity
  wins over prose here.
- Link to the repo and to fuller docs at the end.

## Argument and evidence

- **Challenge the premise before picking a side.** On contested topics (AI, especially),
  Sam's move is to reject the framing and judge case-by-case rather than perform a hot
  take. Argue from stated values — human creativity, the environment, privacy, open
  source, the indie web — but reason, don't preach.
- **Hedge honestly.** *"I think,"* *"I like to think of it as,"* *"it's too early to
  form an opinion"* are features, not weaknesses. Don't inflate uncertainty into
  false confidence.
- Light citation is fine and encouraged (a linked survey, a Wikipedia page), but the bar
  is *interesting and honest*, not *thoroughly cited*. Don't let sourcing become the
  perfectionism trap in disguise.

## Disclosure and values (non-negotiable)

- **Disclose AI assistance.** When an LLM helped produce or shape a post, say so, plainly,
  at the end — as the human-app post does (*"An early draft of this post was created with
  an LLM..."*, *"Scroll animations were developed with the assistance of..."*). This is a
  values commitment: the blog argues for human authorship and oversight, so it has to
  practice it.
- Keep the human in front: the ideas, judgments, and voice are Sam's, whatever tools
  helped along the way.

## Publication readiness

A post is ready when:
- [ ] It says one clear thing, and the reader will know what that thing is.
- [ ] The opening starts concrete, not with warm-up.
- [ ] It's honest — including where it's uncertain, blunt, or personal.
- [ ] Technical posts link to the repo/docs; claims that lean on a source link to it.
- [ ] AI assistance, if any, is disclosed at the end.
- [ ] It's interesting and new — and you're not holding it back for polish it doesn't
      need. **Post it. Boom.**
